Social Anxiety Disorder

What is Social Anxiety Disorder?

Social anxiety disorder is a mental health condition involving intense fear or anxiety in social situations where a person may feel judged, embarrassed, or rejected. The fear can interfere with everyday life.

Symptoms

Symptoms may include intense fear of being watched or judged, avoiding social situations, difficulty speaking with others, sweating, trembling, a racing heartbeat, and strong worry before or after social situations.

Causes and Risk Factors

Social anxiety disorder may result from a combination of genetic, biological, and environmental factors. Shyness, stressful experiences, bullying, and a family history of anxiety disorders may increase the risk.

Diagnosis

A healthcare professional can assess symptoms, social experiences, and how anxiety affects daily life. The assessment also helps distinguish social anxiety from normal feelings of shyness.

Treatment

Treatment may include psychological therapies such as cognitive behavioral therapy. Medicines may also be used in some cases according to the advice of a healthcare professional.

Prevention and Management

Learning ways to manage anxiety, gradually facing feared situations with professional guidance, staying socially connected, exercising regularly, and getting enough sleep may support mental well-being.

When to Seek Help

Professional support is recommended when social fear causes significant distress or makes it difficult to attend school, work, social activities, or maintain relationships.
