Lung Cancer

What is Lung Cancer?

Lung cancer is a type of cancer that begins in the lungs. It happens when abnormal cells grow uncontrollably and can form a tumor. Lung cancer can affect breathing and may spread to other parts of the body if it is not treated.

Symptoms

Common symptoms may include a persistent cough, coughing up blood, chest pain, shortness of breath, unexplained weight loss, tiredness, and repeated respiratory infections. Some people may have no symptoms in the early stages.

Causes and Risk Factors

Smoking is the most important risk factor for lung cancer. Other factors include exposure to secondhand smoke, radon, certain chemicals and substances at work, air pollution, previous radiation to the chest, and a family history of lung cancer.

Diagnosis

Doctors may use a medical history and physical examination together with imaging tests such as a chest X-ray or CT scan. A biopsy may be performed to examine lung tissue and confirm whether cancer is present.

Treatment

Treatment depends on the type and stage of lung cancer and the person's overall health. Treatments may include surgery, radiation therapy, chemotherapy, targeted therapy, or immunotherapy. Doctors may use one treatment or a combination of treatments.

Prevention and Risk Reduction

Not smoking is one of the most important ways to reduce the risk of lung cancer. Avoiding secondhand smoke and harmful workplace substances can also help. Testing homes for radon and reducing exposure when levels are high may further reduce risk.

When to Seek Help

A persistent cough, coughing up blood, unexplained weight loss, chest pain, or ongoing breathing problems should be discussed with a healthcare professional. Early medical evaluation can help identify the cause of these symptoms.
