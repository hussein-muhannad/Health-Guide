Kidney Cancer

What is Kidney Cancer?

Kidney cancer is a type of cancer that begins in the cells of the kidney. The kidneys are two organs that help remove waste and extra fluid from the blood and produce urine. The most common type of kidney cancer in adults is renal cell carcinoma.

Symptoms

Kidney cancer may not cause symptoms in its early stages. Possible symptoms include blood in the urine, pain in the side or lower back that does not go away, a lump or swelling in the side or abdomen, unexplained weight loss, tiredness, fever, or loss of appetite.

Causes and Risk Factors

Risk factors include smoking, being overweight or obese, high blood pressure, certain inherited conditions, a family history of kidney cancer, and long-term exposure to some workplace substances. Risk also generally increases with age.

Diagnosis

Doctors may use blood and urine tests and imaging tests such as ultrasound, CT scans, or MRI. In some cases, a biopsy may be performed to examine tissue and help confirm the diagnosis.

Treatment

Treatment depends on the type and stage of kidney cancer and the person's overall health. Options may include surgery, active surveillance in selected cases, ablation, radiation therapy, targeted therapy, or immunotherapy.

Prevention and Risk Reduction

Not all kidney cancers can be prevented, but some risks can be reduced. Avoiding smoking, maintaining a healthy weight, staying physically active, and managing high blood pressure can support kidney and overall health. Reducing exposure to harmful workplace substances can also help.

When to Seek Help

Blood in the urine, persistent pain in the side or lower back, an unexplained lump or swelling, unexplained weight loss, or ongoing tiredness should be discussed with a healthcare professional.
