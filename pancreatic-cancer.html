Pancreatic Cancer

What is Pancreatic Cancer?

Pancreatic cancer is a type of cancer that begins in the pancreas, an organ located behind the stomach. The pancreas helps the body digest food and produces hormones such as insulin. Pancreatic cancer occurs when abnormal cells grow uncontrollably.

Symptoms

Early pancreatic cancer may not cause clear symptoms. Possible symptoms include yellowing of the skin or eyes, dark urine, abdominal or back pain, unexplained weight loss, loss of appetite, nausea, and changes in bowel movements. Some people may also develop new problems with blood sugar.

Causes and Risk Factors

Risk factors include smoking, increasing age, a family history of pancreatic cancer, certain inherited genetic conditions, chronic pancreatitis, obesity, and diabetes. Having a risk factor does not mean that a person will definitely develop pancreatic cancer.

Diagnosis

Doctors may use blood tests and imaging tests such as CT scans, MRI, or ultrasound to examine the pancreas. Endoscopic procedures may also be used, and a biopsy can help confirm the diagnosis when needed.

Treatment

Treatment depends on the type and stage of pancreatic cancer and the person's overall health. It may include surgery, chemotherapy, radiation therapy, targeted therapy, or other medicines. Treatment may involve more than one approach.

Prevention and Risk Reduction

There is no guaranteed way to prevent pancreatic cancer. Avoiding smoking, maintaining a healthy weight, staying physically active, and managing conditions such as diabetes and chronic pancreatitis can help reduce some risk factors. Limiting alcohol can also support overall health.

When to Seek Help

Persistent abdominal or back pain, unexplained weight loss, yellowing of the skin or eyes, changes in bowel habits, or other unusual symptoms should be discussed with a healthcare professional, especially when they continue or become worse.
