Leukemia

What is Leukemia?

Leukemia is a type of cancer that affects blood-forming tissues, including the bone marrow. It causes abnormal blood cells to grow uncontrollably. There are several types of leukemia, and they can develop differently and affect different types of blood cells.

Symptoms

Symptoms may include tiredness, weakness, frequent infections, fever, easy bruising or bleeding, unexplained weight loss, swollen lymph nodes, and bone or joint pain. Symptoms can vary depending on the type of leukemia.

Causes and Risk Factors

The exact cause of most cases of leukemia is not known. Some risk factors can increase the chance of developing certain types, including exposure to high levels of radiation, some chemicals, previous cancer treatment, certain genetic conditions, and smoking for some forms of leukemia.

Diagnosis

Doctors may begin with a physical examination and blood tests such as a complete blood count. If leukemia is suspected, tests of the bone marrow may be performed. Additional laboratory tests can help identify the specific type of leukemia.

Treatment

Treatment depends on the type of leukemia, its stage or risk group, and the person's overall health. Treatments may include chemotherapy, targeted therapy, immunotherapy, radiation therapy, or stem cell transplantation. Some patients may receive more than one treatment.

Prevention and Risk Reduction

Most cases of leukemia cannot currently be prevented. Avoiding tobacco, following workplace safety guidelines when exposed to harmful substances, and discussing unusual or persistent symptoms with a healthcare professional can support overall health.

When to Seek Help

Persistent tiredness, frequent infections, unexplained bruising or bleeding, ongoing fever, unexplained weight loss, or unusual bone or joint pain should be discussed with a healthcare professional.
