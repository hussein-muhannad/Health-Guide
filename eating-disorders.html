Eating Disorders

What are Eating Disorders?

Eating disorders are serious mental health conditions involving disturbances in eating behavior and thoughts about food, eating, weight, or body shape. They can affect both physical and mental health.

Symptoms

Symptoms vary depending on the type of eating disorder. They may include major changes in eating habits, strong concerns about weight or body shape, avoiding certain foods, overeating, or behaviors intended to change body weight.

Causes and Risk Factors

Eating disorders can develop from a combination of genetic, biological, psychological, and social factors. Family history, body image concerns, stress, and certain life experiences may increase the risk.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis is made by healthcare professionals through an assessment of eating behaviors, thoughts, physical health, and emotional well-being. Medical tests may also be used to check for physical effects.

Treatment

Treatment depends on the type and severity of the eating disorder. It may include psychological therapy, nutritional support, medical care, and sometimes medicines.

Prevention and Management

Early support, healthy attitudes toward food and body image, regular meals, and professional guidance can help. Avoiding harmful dieting practices and seeking help early may also be beneficial.

When to Seek Help

Professional help should be sought if eating habits, concerns about weight or body shape, or related behaviors begin to affect physical health, emotions, relationships, study, or daily life.
