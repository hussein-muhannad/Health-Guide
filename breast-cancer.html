Breast Cancer

What is Breast Cancer?

Breast cancer is a type of cancer that begins in the cells of the breast. It occurs when abnormal cells grow uncontrollably and may form a tumor. Breast cancer can occur in both women and men, although it is much more common in women.

Symptoms

Symptoms may include a new lump or thickening in the breast or underarm, a change in the size or shape of the breast, changes in the skin of the breast, changes in the nipple, or unusual discharge from the nipple. Some people may have no symptoms in the early stages.

Causes and Risk Factors

The risk of breast cancer increases with age. Other risk factors can include a family history of breast cancer, certain inherited genetic changes, previous exposure to radiation, hormonal factors, alcohol use, being overweight after menopause, and physical inactivity.

Diagnosis

Doctors may use a physical examination and imaging tests such as mammography, ultrasound, or MRI. If an abnormal area is found, a biopsy may be performed to examine the cells and confirm whether cancer is present.

Treatment

Treatment depends on the type and stage of breast cancer. It may include surgery, radiation therapy, chemotherapy, hormone therapy, targeted therapy, or immunotherapy. Doctors may use one treatment or a combination of treatments.

Prevention and Risk Reduction

Not all breast cancers can be prevented, but some risk factors can be reduced. Maintaining a healthy weight, staying physically active, limiting alcohol, and avoiding smoking can help support overall health. Following recommended breast cancer screening can also help detect some cancers early.

When to Seek Help

A new breast lump, unexplained changes in the breast or nipple, skin changes, or unusual nipple discharge should be checked by a healthcare professional. Many breast changes are not cancer, but medical evaluation is important to determine the cause.
