Depression

What is Depression?

Depression is a common mental health condition that can cause persistent sadness and loss of interest or pleasure in activities. It can affect how a person feels, thinks, and manages daily life.

Symptoms

Symptoms may include persistent sadness, loss of interest in activities, changes in sleep or appetite, tiredness, difficulty concentrating, feelings of worthlessness, and changes in daily activities.

Causes and Risk Factors

Depression can result from a combination of biological, psychological, and social factors. Stressful life events, family history, and some medical conditions may increase the risk.

Diagnosis

A healthcare professional can assess symptoms, personal history, and their effect on daily life. The assessment also helps identify other conditions that may cause similar symptoms.

Treatment

Depression can be treated. Treatment may include psychological therapies, medicines, or a combination of both. The appropriate treatment depends on the person's condition and needs.

Prevention and Management

Regular physical activity, healthy sleep habits, maintaining social connections, managing stress, and seeking professional support can help with mental well-being and recovery.

When to Seek Help

If symptoms continue for a long time or interfere with daily life, study, work, or relationships, it is important to speak with a qualified healthcare professional.
