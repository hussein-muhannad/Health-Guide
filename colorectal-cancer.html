Colorectal Cancer

What is Colorectal Cancer?

Colorectal cancer is a type of cancer that begins in the colon or rectum. It usually develops from abnormal growths called polyps, which can sometimes become cancerous over time. Early detection and treatment can improve outcomes.

Symptoms

Symptoms may include changes in bowel habits, blood in the stool, abdominal pain or discomfort, unexplained weight loss, weakness, and tiredness. Some people may not have symptoms in the early stages.

Causes and Risk Factors

Risk factors include increasing age, a family history of colorectal cancer or certain polyps, some inherited genetic conditions, inflammatory bowel diseases, physical inactivity, being overweight, smoking, alcohol use, and diets high in processed or red meat.

Diagnosis

Doctors may use medical history, physical examination, stool tests, and other tests to look for signs of colorectal cancer. Colonoscopy is commonly used to examine the inside of the colon and rectum. A biopsy may be performed when an abnormal area is found.

Treatment

Treatment depends on the location and stage of the cancer. It may include surgery, chemotherapy, radiation therapy, targeted therapy, or immunotherapy. Some patients may receive a combination of treatments.

Prevention and Risk Reduction

Regular physical activity, maintaining a healthy weight, avoiding smoking, limiting alcohol, and eating a healthy diet can help reduce risk. Screening can also find some colorectal cancers early or detect polyps before they become cancerous.

When to Seek Help

Blood in the stool, persistent changes in bowel habits, unexplained weight loss, ongoing abdominal pain, or unusual tiredness should be discussed with a healthcare professional. These symptoms can have different causes, but they should be evaluated when they persist or are concerning.
