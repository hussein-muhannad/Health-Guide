Panic Disorder

What is Panic Disorder?

Panic disorder is a mental health condition in which a person experiences repeated and unexpected panic attacks. These attacks can cause intense fear and physical symptoms even when there is no clear danger.

Symptoms

Panic attacks may involve a racing heartbeat, sweating, trembling, shortness of breath, dizziness, chest discomfort, fear of losing control, or a strong feeling that something terrible is about to happen.

Causes and Risk Factors

The exact causes are not fully understood. Genetic factors, major stress, traumatic experiences, and changes in the way the brain responds to fear may contribute to the condition.

Diagnosis

A healthcare professional can assess the person's symptoms, medical history, and the pattern of panic attacks. A medical evaluation may also be used to rule out other conditions that can cause similar symptoms.

Treatment

Panic disorder can be treated. Treatment may include cognitive behavioral therapy, medicines, or a combination of treatments depending on the person's condition and needs.

Prevention and Management

Following a treatment plan, learning healthy ways to manage stress, maintaining regular sleep, staying physically active, and avoiding excessive caffeine may help some people manage symptoms.

When to Seek Help

If panic attacks happen repeatedly or cause a person to avoid normal activities, school, work, or social situations, it is important to speak with a qualified healthcare professional.
