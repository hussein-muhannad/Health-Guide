Bipolar Disorder

What is Bipolar Disorder?

Bipolar disorder is a mental health condition that causes noticeable changes in mood, energy, activity levels, and the ability to carry out daily tasks. People may experience periods of elevated or irritable mood and periods of depression.

Symptoms

Symptoms can vary depending on the mood episode. During manic or hypomanic episodes, a person may have increased energy, reduced need for sleep, rapid speech, or increased activity. During depressive episodes, sadness, low energy, and loss of interest may occur.

Causes and Risk Factors

The exact cause of bipolar disorder is not fully known. Research suggests that genetic, biological, and environmental factors may contribute to its development. Having a close family member with bipolar disorder may increase the risk.

Diagnosis

A healthcare professional diagnoses bipolar disorder by evaluating symptoms, mood changes, personal history, and family history. The assessment may also help rule out other conditions with similar symptoms.

Treatment

Treatment can help manage bipolar disorder. It may include medicines such as mood stabilizers and psychological therapies. Treatment is usually planned according to the person's symptoms and needs.

Prevention and Management

Following a treatment plan, maintaining regular sleep, managing stress, avoiding alcohol and drugs, and keeping regular appointments with healthcare professionals can help manage the condition.

When to Seek Help

Anyone experiencing major changes in mood, sleep, energy, or behavior that affect daily life should speak with a qualified healthcare professional.
