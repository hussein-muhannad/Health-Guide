Prostate Cancer

What is Prostate Cancer?

Prostate cancer is a type of cancer that begins in the prostate gland. The prostate is a small gland in the male reproductive system that helps produce part of the fluid found in semen. Some prostate cancers grow slowly, while others can grow and spread more quickly.

Symptoms

Early prostate cancer often causes no symptoms. When symptoms occur, they may include difficulty starting or maintaining urination, weak or interrupted urine flow, frequent urination, blood in the urine or semen, and pain in the back, hips, or pelvis.

Causes and Risk Factors

The risk of prostate cancer increases with age. Other risk factors include a family history of prostate cancer and certain inherited genetic changes. Race and ethnicity can also be associated with differences in risk.

Diagnosis

Doctors may evaluate symptoms and medical history and may perform a physical examination. Tests can include a prostate-specific antigen (PSA) blood test and other examinations. If cancer is suspected, a biopsy may be used to confirm the diagnosis.

Treatment

Treatment depends on the cancer's stage, grade, and the person's overall health. Options may include active surveillance, surgery, radiation therapy, hormone therapy, chemotherapy, or other medicines. Some patients may need more than one type of treatment.

Prevention and Risk Reduction

There is no guaranteed way to prevent prostate cancer. Maintaining a healthy weight, being physically active, eating a balanced diet, and discussing personal risk with a healthcare professional can support overall health. Screening decisions should be discussed with a doctor based on age and individual risk.

When to Seek Help

Problems with urination, blood in the urine or semen, persistent pain in the back, hips, or pelvis, or other unusual urinary symptoms should be discussed with a healthcare professional. These symptoms can have several causes, so medical evaluation is important.
