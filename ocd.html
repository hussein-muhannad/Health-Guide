Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder (OCD)

What is OCD?

Obsessive-compulsive disorder is a mental health condition involving recurring unwanted thoughts, images, or urges called obsessions, and repetitive behaviors or mental acts called compulsions.

Symptoms

Symptoms may include unwanted repeated thoughts, fears about contamination or harm, repeated checking, cleaning, counting, arranging, or performing certain actions to reduce anxiety.

Causes and Risk Factors

The exact cause of OCD is not fully understood. Genetic, biological, and environmental factors may contribute. A family history of OCD may also increase the risk.

Diagnosis

A healthcare professional diagnoses OCD by asking about thoughts, behaviors, symptoms, and how much they interfere with everyday life. Other conditions may also be considered during the assessment.

Treatment

OCD can be treated. Common treatments include cognitive behavioral therapy, especially a form called exposure and response prevention, and certain medicines when appropriate.

Prevention and Management

There is no guaranteed way to prevent OCD. Following a treatment plan, attending therapy sessions, learning ways to manage anxiety, and maintaining healthy daily habits can help manage symptoms.

When to Seek Help

Professional help should be considered when unwanted thoughts or repetitive behaviors take up a lot of time or interfere with school, work, relationships, or everyday activities.
