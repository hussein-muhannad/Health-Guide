Liver Cancer

What is Liver Cancer?

Liver cancer is a type of cancer that begins in the cells of the liver. The most common primary liver cancer in adults is hepatocellular carcinoma. Cancer can also spread to the liver from other parts of the body, which is called secondary or metastatic cancer.

Symptoms

Possible symptoms include unexplained weight loss, loss of appetite, feeling full after eating a small amount, pain or discomfort in the upper right part of the abdomen, nausea, weakness, yellowing of the skin or eyes, and swelling of the abdomen.

Causes and Risk Factors

Important risk factors include chronic infection with hepatitis B or hepatitis C, cirrhosis of the liver, heavy alcohol use, certain inherited conditions, and exposure to aflatoxins. Metabolic conditions such as fatty liver disease can also increase risk.

Diagnosis

Doctors may use blood tests and imaging tests such as ultrasound, CT scans, or MRI to examine the liver. In some cases, a biopsy may be performed to examine liver tissue and confirm the diagnosis.

Treatment

Treatment depends on the type and stage of liver cancer and the condition of the liver. Options may include surgery, liver transplantation in selected cases, ablation, embolization, radiation therapy, targeted therapy, or immunotherapy.

Prevention and Risk Reduction

Hepatitis B vaccination can help prevent hepatitis B infection. Reducing alcohol use, preventing hepatitis infections, maintaining a healthy weight, and managing conditions that affect the liver can help reduce some risk factors. People with chronic liver disease may need regular medical monitoring.

When to Seek Help

Unexplained weight loss, persistent abdominal discomfort, yellowing of the skin or eyes, abdominal swelling, or other unusual liver-related symptoms should be discussed with a healthcare professional, especially in people with known liver disease.
