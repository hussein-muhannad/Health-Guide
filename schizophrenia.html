Schizophrenia

What is Schizophrenia?

Schizophrenia is a serious mental health condition that can affect how a person thinks, feels, and behaves. It may affect a person's ability to understand reality and carry out everyday activities.

Symptoms

Symptoms may include hallucinations, delusions, disorganized thinking or speech, reduced motivation, and difficulties with concentration and daily functioning. Symptoms can vary from person to person.

Causes and Risk Factors

The exact cause is not fully understood. Genetic, biological, and environmental factors may contribute to the development of schizophrenia. Having a family history of the condition may increase the risk.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis is made by a qualified mental health professional through a detailed assessment of symptoms, behavior, medical history, and how the condition affects daily life.

Treatment

Treatment may include antipsychotic medicines, psychological therapies, rehabilitation, and social support. A combination of treatments can help many people manage symptoms and improve their quality of life.

Prevention and Management

There is no guaranteed way to prevent schizophrenia. Early recognition of symptoms, continuing treatment, avoiding harmful substances, and having regular professional support can help manage the condition.

When to Seek Help

If a person experiences hallucinations, unusual beliefs, severe confusion, major changes in behavior, or serious difficulties with daily life, professional help should be sought as soon as possible.
