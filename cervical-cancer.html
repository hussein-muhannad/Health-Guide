Cervical Cancer

What is Cervical Cancer?

Cervical cancer is a type of cancer that begins in the cells of the cervix, which is the lower part of the uterus that connects to the vagina. Most cervical cancers are caused by persistent infection with certain types of human papillomavirus (HPV).

Symptoms

Early cervical cancer may not cause noticeable symptoms. Possible symptoms include unusual vaginal bleeding, bleeding after sexual intercourse, bleeding between periods, unusual vaginal discharge, or pelvic pain. Symptoms can also occur in other conditions.

Causes and Risk Factors

Persistent infection with high-risk types of HPV is the main cause of cervical cancer. Other factors that can increase risk include smoking, a weakened immune system, and certain other conditions that make it harder for the body to clear HPV infection.

Diagnosis

Cervical cancer can be detected through screening tests such as HPV testing and cervical cytology, commonly known as a Pap test. If an abnormal result is found, further examinations and a biopsy may be needed to determine whether cancer is present.

Treatment

Treatment depends on the stage and type of cervical cancer. It may include surgery, radiation therapy, chemotherapy, targeted therapy, or immunotherapy. Some patients may receive a combination of treatments.

Prevention and Risk Reduction

HPV vaccination is an important way to help prevent cervical cancer. Regular cervical cancer screening can help detect HPV infection or abnormal cells before cancer develops. Avoiding smoking and following recommended medical care can also help reduce risk.

When to Seek Help

Unusual vaginal bleeding, bleeding after sexual intercourse, unusual discharge, or persistent pelvic pain should be discussed with a healthcare professional. Regular screening is also important because cervical cancer can develop before symptoms appear.
