Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD)

What is PTSD?

Post-traumatic stress disorder is a mental health condition that can develop after experiencing or witnessing a frightening, dangerous, or traumatic event. It can affect a person's thoughts, feelings, and behavior.

Symptoms

Symptoms may include unwanted memories or nightmares, avoiding reminders of the event, feeling constantly alert or on edge, difficulty sleeping, and changes in mood or thinking.

Causes and Risk Factors

PTSD can occur after traumatic experiences such as serious accidents, violence, natural disasters, war, or other threatening events. Not everyone who experiences trauma develops PTSD.

Diagnosis

A healthcare professional can assess symptoms, personal history, and the effect of the traumatic experience on daily life. The assessment helps determine whether the symptoms meet the criteria for PTSD.

Treatment

PTSD can be treated. Treatments may include trauma-focused psychological therapies and, in some cases, medicines. A healthcare professional can help choose an appropriate treatment plan.

Prevention and Management

After a traumatic event, support from trusted people, maintaining regular routines, getting enough sleep, and seeking professional help when symptoms persist can support recovery.

When to Seek Help

Professional help should be sought when symptoms continue, become severe, or interfere with work, study, relationships, sleep, or everyday activities.
