Stomach Cancer

What is Stomach Cancer?

Stomach cancer, also called gastric cancer, is a type of cancer that begins in the stomach. It develops when abnormal cells grow uncontrollably and form a tumor. Different types of stomach cancer can develop in different parts of the stomach.

Symptoms

Early stomach cancer may cause few or no symptoms. Possible symptoms include indigestion, stomach discomfort or pain, feeling full after eating a small amount, nausea, vomiting, difficulty swallowing, unexplained weight loss, and tiredness.

Causes and Risk Factors

Risk factors include infection with Helicobacter pylori (H. pylori), chronic inflammation of the stomach, smoking, a family history of stomach cancer, certain inherited conditions, and diets high in salted or smoked foods. Risk also generally increases with age.

Diagnosis

Doctors may review symptoms and medical history and perform a physical examination. An upper endoscopy can be used to examine the stomach. During the procedure, a biopsy may be taken to examine the tissue for cancer cells. Imaging tests may also be used to determine whether the cancer has spread.

Treatment

Treatment depends on the type and stage of stomach cancer. It may include surgery, chemotherapy, radiation therapy, targeted therapy, or immunotherapy. Treatment may involve more than one approach depending on the individual case.

Prevention and Risk Reduction

Some risk factors cannot be changed, but certain steps may help reduce risk. Treating H. pylori infection when present, avoiding smoking, eating a balanced diet with plenty of fruits and vegetables, and limiting heavily salted or smoked foods can support stomach health.

When to Seek Help

Persistent stomach pain, ongoing indigestion, difficulty swallowing, repeated vomiting, unexplained weight loss, blood in the stool or vomit, or feeling unusually full after small meals should be discussed with a healthcare professional.
